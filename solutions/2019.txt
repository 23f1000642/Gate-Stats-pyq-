=== 2019 11
topic: CAL
pattern: Limit of a sum as a Riemann integral
type: MCQ
ans: D
concept: A limit of the form $\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^{n} f\!\left(\frac kn\right)$ is a Riemann sum, and equals $\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx$.
solution:
1. Take $n^2$ out of the denominator: $\dfrac{n}{n^2+k^2}=\dfrac{1}{n}\cdot\dfrac{1}{1+(k/n)^2}$.
2. So the sum is $\dfrac1n\sum_{k=1}^n f(k/n)$ with $f(x)=\dfrac{1}{1+x^2}$.
3. Replace $\frac1n\to dx$, $\frac kn\to x$, and the range $k=1..n$ becomes $x\in(0,1]$:
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{n}{n^2+k^2}=\int_0^1\frac{dx}{1+x^2}=\tan^{-1}x\Big|_0^1=\frac{\pi}{4}.$$
shortcut: Recipe: (i) pull out $\frac1n$, (ii) write everything else in terms of $r=k/n$, (iii) the limits of $x$ are $\lim k/n$ at the lower and upper ends. If the sum runs to $2n$, the upper limit becomes 2.
trap: Do not try to sum term by term. Also check that exactly one factor $\frac1n$ comes out. If $\frac{1}{n^2}$ comes out, the limit is 0.

topic: CAL
pattern: Line integral via Green's theorem
type: MCQ
ans: B
concept: **Green's theorem.** For a positively oriented (anticlockwise) closed curve $C$ in the plane, $\oint_C P\,dx+Q\,dy=\iint_R\left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right)dA$.
solution:
1. The triangle lies in the plane $z=0$, so $\vec F\cdot d\vec r=(x-y)(dx+dy)$. That gives $P=Q=x-y$.
2. $(0,0)\to(5,0)\to(5,5)\to(0,0)$ goes anticlockwise, so it is positive orientation.
3. $\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}=1-(-1)=2$.
4. The area of the triangle is $\frac12\cdot5\cdot5=12.5$, so the integral is $2\times12.5=25$.

shortcut: When $\partial Q/\partial x-\partial P/\partial y$ is a constant $c$, the answer is $c\times$ area. Only the sign needs care: clockwise flips it.

pattern: Dimension of a subspace of matrices
type: MCQ
ans: D
concept: The dimension equals the number of free parameters. Each independent linear constraint lowers it by 1.
solution:

=== 2019 17
topic: EST
pattern: Basu's theorem: complete sufficient vs ancillary
type: MCQ
ans: A
concept: **Basu's theorem.** A complete sufficient statistic is independent of every ancillary statistic. An ancillary statistic is one whose distribution does not depend on $\theta$.
solution:
1. For $U(0,\theta)$, $X_{(n)}$ is complete and sufficient for $\theta$.
2. Write $X_i=\theta U_i$ with $U_i\sim U(0,1)$. Then $\dfrac{X_{(1)}}{X_{(n)}}=\dfrac{U_{(1)}}{U_{(n)}}$ is free of $\theta$, so it is ancillary.
3. By Basu, $X_{(n)}$ and $X_{(1)}/X_{(n)}$ are independent, so their covariance is $0$.
shortcut: In a **scale family**, any ratio of two scale-equivariant statistics, such as $X_{(1)}/X_{(n)}$ or $X_i/\sum X_j$, is ancillary. In a **location family**, any difference ($X_{(n)}-X_{(1)}$, $X_i-\bar X$) is ancillary. Combined with a complete sufficient statistic, the answer is independence and zero covariance.
trap: Options (b) to (d) are not even dimensionally consistent: covariance must scale like $\theta$ times a dimensionless quantity, i.e. $\theta^1$.

Minimise $\sum\frac{(Y_i-\beta)^2}{\sigma_i^2}$. Setting the derivative to 0 gives $\hat\beta=\dfrac{\sum Y_i/\sigma_i^2}{\sum1/\sigma_i^2}$. Its variance is $\frac1{\sum1/\sigma_i^2}$, the minimum among linear unbiased estimators.
shortcut: Minimise $\operatorname{Var}(\sum c_iY_i)=\sum c_i^2\sigma_i^2$ subject to $\sum c_i=1$. Lagrange gives $c_i\propto1/\sigma_i^2$.
